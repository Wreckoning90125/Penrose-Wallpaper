# Octagonal tilings — 8-fold

The 8-fold family: the canonical aperiodic Ammann–Beenker tiling; the
Harriss–Lamb characterisation of which cut-and-project tilings of that type
also admit substitution rules; and John Savard's Keplerian octagonal
tessellation built by an explicit recurrence. Per-section References record
each system's sources.

---

## Ammann–Beenker tiling

**Summary.** The standard aperiodic 8-fold tiling — the octagonal counterpart
of the Penrose tiling. A square and a 45° rhombus with edge-matching rules.

| Property         | Value |
|------------------|-------|
| Symmetry order   | 8-fold |
| Symmetry type    | statistical |
| Aperiodic        | yes |
| Prototiles       | 2 — square, 45°/135° rhombus |
| Construction     | substitution + matching-rule (also cut-and-project) |
| Inflation factor | the silver ratio 1 + √2 |
| Attribution      | Robert Ammann; F. P. M. Beenker |

### Prototiles
A square and a rhombus with 45°/135° angles, equal edge length. (The source
PDFs reference Ammann–Beenker only as the canonical aperiodic octagonal
tiling, by way of contrast with Savard's Keplerian construction below; the
prototile detail here is the standard definition, not from the source.)

### Construction
A substitution tiling with inflation factor the silver ratio 1 + √2; also
constructible by cut-and-project from the 4-dimensional integer lattice.
(Detailed child layout not given in the source PDFs.)

### Matching rules
Edge matching rules force aperiodicity; Ammann bars exist for it (the
octagonal analogue of the Penrose Ammann bars in `pentagonal-penrose.md`).

### Symmetry & aperiodicity
Genuinely aperiodic; statistical 8-fold. The standard reference point for
"what a real aperiodic octagonal tiling looks like."

### Variants & relations
The aperiodic counterpart against which Savard's Keplerian octagonal
tessellation (below) is contrasted — the Keplerian one is a *recurrence*
tiling, not a matching-rule aperiodic tiling.

### History & decoration
Named for Robert Ammann and F. P. M. Beenker, independent discoverers.

### Renderer mapping
Implemented — `Family::AmmannBeenker`. Generated by `generateMultigrid(4, …)`
in `tiling/penrose.cpp`: the de Bruijn dualization of four line grids 45°
apart yields exactly this tiling's square + 45° rhomb (`type` 0 = rhomb,
1 = square). The ripple shader uses `waveSymmetry` 8. The renderer ships the
de Bruijn dualization rather than the silver-ratio substitution — both
generate the same Ammann–Beenker tiling.

### References
Robert Ammann; F. P. M. Beenker; N. G. de Bruijn, "Algebraic theory of
Penrose's non-periodic tilings of the plane", *Nederl. Akad. Wetensch. Proc.
Ser. A* **84** (1981) 38–66 (the dualization method, here run on four line
grids — see Renderer mapping); quadibloc.

## Harriss–Lamb canonical substitution tilings

**Summary.** The cut-and-project tilings that also admit an exact substitution
rule. Harriss and Lamb characterised them completely: a canonical projection
tiling has a substitution rule precisely when its projection data are the
eigenspaces of an integer "quadratic expansion matrix." Ammann–Beenker is the
8-fold example; the same theory yields six further tilings of its type.

| Property         | Value |
|------------------|-------|
| Symmetry order   | 8-fold (the 4→2 Ammann–Beenker projection family) |
| Symmetry type    | statistical |
| Aperiodic        | yes |
| Prototiles       | square + 45°/135° rhomb (Ammann–Beenker type), edge-labelled |
| Construction     | cut-and-project that also admits a substitution rule |
| Inflation factor | a quadratic unit — 1+√2 for Ammann–Beenker; 2+√5, 7+√53, 2+√3, 3+2√2 across the six further examples |
| Attribution      | Edmund O. Harriss; Jeroen S. W. Lamb |

### Prototiles
The Ammann–Beenker square and 45°/135° rhomb, each carried as several
*labelled* prototiles — one per rotation and per reflection — so the
substitution, which uses translations only, can still place every orientation
that is needed. Ammann–Beenker itself uses the rhomb in 4 rotations and the
(curve-marked) square in 8: twelve labelled prototiles. A tile is the
parallelogram spanned by two edge vectors; the edge vectors of a 4→2 canonical
tiling generate a ℤ-module isomorphic to ℤ⁴.

### Construction
Two equivalent descriptions, joined by one theorem.

*Cut-and-project.* Slice the integer lattice ℤⁿ by a strip V ⊕ window and
project the lattice points inside the strip onto the physical plane V — the
result is a canonical projection tiling. ℝⁿ splits as V ⊕ W ⊕ R: V the
physical plane, W the internal space carrying the acceptance window, R a
residual space.

*Substitution.* A rule replacing each tile by a patch of λ-times-smaller
tiles. A canonical projection tiling that also admits such a rule, the rule
respecting the vertex hierarchy, is a **canonical substitution tiling**.

*Characterisation* (Harriss, thesis Theorem 2). A canonical projection tiling
admits a substitution rule **iff** V, W and R are the eigenspaces of an
integer **quadratic expansion matrix** M — a matrix fixing each of V, W, R and
satisfying a lattice "tiling property." The substitution's linear scale λ is
then the expanding eigenvalue of M, and is necessarily a **quadratic unit** (a
quadratic algebraic integer whose conjugate has modulus < 1; thesis Lemma
5.2.3). A single tiling admits a countable infinity of distinct substitution
rules — one per admissible matrix power Mᵐ and per choice of acceptance
subwindow. For Ammann–Beenker the expansion matrix is

    M = [  1  1  0 −1 ]
        [  1  1  1  0 ]
        [  0  1  1  1 ]
        [ −1  0  1  1 ]

with det 1 and expanding eigenvalue λ = 1 + √2.

### Matching rules
None beyond the substitution. Being cut-and-project tilings they also admit
local matching rules (the Ammann–Beenker bars / edge markings); the
substitution rule and the matching rule generate the same tiling.

### Symmetry & aperiodicity
Statistical 8-fold. Aperiodic — a projection through an irrational plane has
no period. The substitution uses a translation-only finite proto-set, so
rotations are excluded: tilings whose tiles take infinitely many orientations
(the pinwheel) fall outside this framework. The quadratic-unit condition on λ
is the sharp boundary of the framework — a similarity substitution tiling
whose scale factor has algebraic degree > 2, such as the heptagonal Harriss
rhomb substitution (see `heptagonal.md`; λ a cubic number), **cannot** be a
cut-and-project tiling.

### Variants & relations
- **Six further examples.** Harriss & Lamb (2004) exhibit six new 4→2
  canonical substitution tilings of Ammann–Beenker type, given by integer
  expansion matrices M₁…M₆ with scaling factors 2+√5, 7+√53, 2+√3 and 3+2√2 —
  distinct 8-fold-family substitution tilings on the Ammann–Beenker
  square + rhomb proto-set.
- **Penrose.** The same theory characterises every substitution rule of the
  Penrose tiling: each is associated with a power Mᵐ of the 5×5 matrix whose
  rows are cyclic shifts of (1,1,0,0,1) (thesis Theorem 4). See
  `pentagonal-penrose.md`.
- **The line.** The 1-D case is the Fibonacci / Sturmian interval tilings;
  thesis Theorem 1 characterises every 2→1 interval substitution rule and
  shows that some are *non-local*.
- **Polytope windows.** A projection tiling with convex ℚ-module polytope
  windows and the expansion property always has a substitution rule (thesis
  Theorem 5).
- Contrast the renderer's de Bruijn multigrid generator (`generateMultigrid`):
  it builds the *projection* side of these tilings directly, with no
  substitution rule attached.

### History & decoration
Edmund Harriss's 2003 Imperial College PhD thesis "On Canonical Substitution
Tilings" (supervisor J. S. W. Lamb) settled which cut-and-project tilings
admit substitution rules; the Ammann–Beenker-type case was published with
Lamb in 2004. The result connects two construction methods — de Bruijn's
projection method (1981) and substitution rules — previously studied apart.

### Renderer mapping
Partially implemented. The Ammann–Beenker tiling itself ships as
`Family::AmmannBeenker`, generated by `generateMultigrid(4, …)` — the
projection (multigrid) side. The substitution side is not implemented: the
thesis gives the expansion matrices but the tile-level child dissection only
as figures (thesis Figs 5.6, 5.13, 5.14), so the M₁…M₆ tilings cannot be
generated from the documented data without first reconstructing each rule's
child geometry. Each would be a distinct 8-fold substitution `Family` sharing
the Ammann–Beenker proto-set; `waveSymmetry` 8.

- E. O. Harriss, "On Canonical Substitution Tilings," PhD thesis, Imperial

- N. G. de Bruijn, "Algebraic theory of Penrose's non-periodic tilings of the

## Keplerian octagonal tessellation

**Summary.** John Savard's original 8-fold tiling built from clusters of
small octagons and connector squares, generated by an explicit recurrence.
A *substitution* tessellation, not a matching-rule aperiodic tiling.

| Property         | Value |
|------------------|-------|
| Symmetry order   | 8-fold (4-fold for the base prototiles individually) |
| Symmetry type    | single-centre |
| Aperiodic        | no — a Keplerian substitution tessellation |
| Prototiles       | octagon (3 colour-roles), diamond, wrench, small square; minor hexagons |
| Construction     | substitution, with overlapping outer octagon rows |
| Inflation factor | not stated numerically |
| Attribution      | John Savard (octagon enlargement after the "Goethe-type" tiling) |

### Prototiles
Every prototile is itself a cluster of small octagons plus small connector
squares ("diamonds" of corner-to-corner small squares, yellow):
- **Octagon** — three roles: **green** (octagons mostly connected diagonally
  to neighbours), **blue-green** (connected orthogonally), **light blue**
  (fully 8-fold symmetric). A "+" vs "X" mark distinguishes the two octagon
  types so a 45° rotation swaps them. Types A, B; a third, C, has true 8-fold
  symmetry.
- **Diamond** (blue) — an elongated rhombic piece; the hardest piece to give
  a recurrence; has only border tiles.
- **Wrench** (purple) — a piece Savard had to invent specifically to make the
  diamond's recurrence work; at most 4-fold symmetry; types A, B.
- **Small square** (yellow) — connector tiles, label C.
- Minor hexagons and other low-priority shapes.
Parity-reversed forms: light blue = reversed diamond; light purple = reversed
wrench.

### Construction
Each prototile expands into a cluster of small octagons + squares + sub-pieces:
- **Octagon** — enlarges into an octagonal cluster of small octagons,
  following the earlier "Goethe-type" octagonal tiling.
- **Diamond** — its recurrence *requires* the wrench. Inside the wrench's
  recurrence, **three diamonds appear in a sheared L-shape**; this works only
  if **one of the three is reflected along its long axis**, so non-octagon
  pieces overlap only octagons.
- **Wrench** — when a sheared-L occurs higher up, the internal arrangement of
  its central wrench is also reversed (parity propagation).
- **Overlap** — unlike the pentagonal Keplerian tessellation, the **outer row
  of octagons of adjacent expanded pieces overlap** (coloured and blank
  octagons alike).
- The chart's bottom-row recurrence rules are supplementary but **required**
  to actually achieve overall 8-fold symmetry; the non-bottom-row rules alone
  only tessellate space (4-fold result).

### Matching rules
A strict **priority hierarchy**: diamonds override octagons; octagons
override wrenches, hexagons, and other shapes. Non-octagon pieces in the outer
row override the octagons they overlap on adjacent pieces.
- **Octagon-overlap resolution.** Where a green octagon overlaps a blue-green
  one, the preferred rule substitutes a fully-symmetric **light-blue octagon**
  in that position (rather than deciding by diagonal-vs-orthogonal connection).
- **Reflection rule.** Sheared-L diamonds must be reflected so non-octagons
  overlap only octagons.
- **+/X mark** encodes the two octagon types so 45° rotation switches colour.
- **Tile-colour rules** (for the final coloured tiles, separate from
  recurrence-guide colours): Rule X — border octagons shared by two
  larger-order tiles get a special colour (this is the rule for the diamond,
  which has only border tiles); Rule C — small squares get their own colour;
  Rules A/B — other shapes keep, or reverse, the green/blue-green recurrence
  colours depending on orientation.

### Symmetry & aperiodicity
Not aperiodic, and not claimed to be — a Keplerian self-similar recurrence
tessellation. Overall 8-fold symmetry holds **only about the special
8-fold-symmetric octagon seed**; an ordinary octagon seed gives only 4-fold,
and other seeds give other symmetries (including an octagonal pinwheel).

### Variants & relations
- **Octagonal pinwheel** — same rules, different seed.
- **Wrench-free** — the diamond is modified so the less-symmetrical wrench is
  unnecessary; costs one border octagon and forces a diamond-orientation rule.
- **Multi-scale** — applying the recurrence so a larger component unit is used
  at each larger scale gives another 8-fold tessellation (the same idea works
  for a pentagonal Keplerian tessellation — a Goethe pentagon tiling exists).
- **Enlarged-wrench** and **odd-side octagon** (a more symmetrical octagon
  whose side spans an odd number of small octagons) variants.

### History & decoration
"Keplerian" = Kepler's self-similar recurrence technique. Octagon enlargement
follows the simpler "Goethe-type" octagonal tiling. The page links onward to
the Ammann–Beenker tiling as the genuine aperiodic counterpart.

### Renderer mapping
Not implemented; not recommended as a `Family`. The priority hierarchy,
parity propagation, invented wrench piece, and seed-dependent symmetry make
it far heavier than the renderer's substitution engine. If an 8-fold family
is wanted, implement **Ammann–Beenker** instead — two tiles, clean
substitution, genuinely aperiodic.

### References
Johannes Kepler (recurrence technique); "Goethe-type" octagonal/pentagonal
tilings; Ammann–Beenker (linked counterpart); quadibloc.
