# Pentagonal tilings — Keplerian substitution tilings

Decorative 5-fold tilings built by *strict recurrence* rather than matching
rules: Kepler's pentagon/star/boat family, John Savard's boat-elimination
work, and the open "pentagons and stars alone" problem. These are
substitution tilings, not Penrose tilings — they do not obey Penrose matching
rules and are not aperiodic in the Penrose sense.

Source: `quadibloc.com/math/pen03.htm`, `pen04.htm`.

---

## Keplerian pentagon / star / boat tiling

**Summary.** The classical decorative 5-fold tiling family attributed to
Kepler: pentagons, stars, boats, decagons, generated by a recurrence in which
every piece inflates into a fixed cluster of child pieces.

| Property         | Value |
|------------------|-------|
| Symmetry order   | 5-fold |
| Symmetry type    | statistical (but see caveat below) |
| Aperiodic        | no — a strict substitution tiling |
| Prototiles       | pentagon, five-point star, boat, diamond (thin rhomb); also decagon, double-size pentagon, "hat", "fat star" |
| Construction     | substitution |
| Inflation factor | golden-ratio family |
| Attribution      | Johannes Kepler |

### Prototiles
Pentagon (5-fold symmetric); star (5-fold); **boat** (a 3-point partial star,
**not** 5-fold symmetric — the piece every variant tries to eliminate);
**diamond** (thin rhomb, "completely unavoidable"); decagon; double-size
pentagon; plus a "hat" piece and a "fat star" in extended treatments.

### Construction
A recurrence: each piece, in one generation, enlarges into a fixed cluster of
child pieces — any patch lies inside some basic tile acting as a parent. In
the standard version an enlarged diamond is oriented with its boat toward the
parent pentagon; the boat-delaying variant orients the enlarged diamond's
star toward the parent instead. Double-size pentagons are added to
twice-enlarged boats and stars to cut boat count.

### Matching rules
None of Penrose type. Assembly follows the recurrence directly; pieces fit by
shape under Kepler-tiling rules.

### Symmetry & aperiodicity
A strict substitution tiling, **not** aperiodic in the Penrose sense. Like
Penrose tilings it usually has the property that any finite patch recurs
infinitely (every patch sits inside some parent tile) — **but** the distance
to a patch's reappearance depends strongly on the patch's location and can be
immensely, unboundedly large. This is the defining contrast with Penrose
tilings, whose reappearance distance is bounded by a low multiple of patch
size.

### Variants & relations
P1, P2, and P3 can all be expressed in terms of these pentagon/star/boat
shapes; P1 is the closest. See the boat-eliminated variant and the
"pentagons and stars alone" problem below. An octagonal analogue exists (see
`octagonal.md`).

### History & decoration
Johannes Kepler's polygon tilings. The whole decorative tradition the Penrose
tilings grew out of.

### Renderer mapping
Not implemented. A Keplerian pentagonal `Family` is feasible — it is a pure
substitution with no matching-rule state — but its unbounded
patch-reappearance distance makes it visually less "uniform" than P3/P2 at a
given zoom. Lower priority than the dodecagonal families.

### References
Johannes Kepler; quadibloc `pen03.htm`.

## Boat-eliminated pentagonal tiling

**Summary.** John Savard's modified Keplerian tiling that removes the
asymmetric boat piece (and the "hat" piece) by substituting in decagons and
double-size pentagons, at the cost of mathematical elegance.

| Property         | Value |
|------------------|-------|
| Symmetry order   | 5-fold |
| Symmetry type    | statistical |
| Aperiodic        | no — decorative substitution tiling |
| Prototiles       | pentagon, star, diamond, double-size pentagon, decagon (boat and hat eliminated) |
| Construction     | substitution (boat removal applied ad hoc) |
| Inflation factor | golden-ratio family |
| Attribution      | John Savard |

### Prototiles
Pentagon, star, diamond, double-size pentagon, decagon. The boat and "hat"
pieces are introduced and then eliminated; only the diamond — not 5-fold
symmetric but unavoidable — remains as a non-pentagonal shape.

### Construction
From a P-like base: (1) enlarge diamonds star-toward-parent to delay boats;
(2) add double-size pentagons to twice-enlarged boats/stars to cut boat
count; (3) cut "hat" pieces with more double-size pentagons; (4) eliminate
remaining hats by introducing decagons. Boat removal was done **piece by
piece on the finished drawing**, not by modifying the underlying recurrence.

### Matching rules
None of Penrose type — recurrence-based assembly.

### Symmetry & aperiodicity
Not aperiodic; a decorative substitution tiling. Savard explicitly notes it
"lacks the mathematical elegance of the Penrose tiling."

### Variants & relations
Because boat removal was ad hoc, the diagram is **not** a proof the boat can
always be eliminated — though a genuine proof does follow from building the
pieces into kite-and-dart tiles that tile the whole plane (see
`pentagonal-penrose.md`). A boat-only tiling (resembling fish scales) exists.
Introducing decagons removes all hats but lets decagons dominate, which is
aesthetically weaker.

### History & decoration
Savard's decorative reworking of Kepler's tiling; aims at a pattern of purely
5-fold-symmetric pieces.

### Renderer mapping
Not implemented; not recommended. The ad-hoc boat removal means there is no
clean substitution rule to encode — it would have to be a stored finite patch
rather than a generative `Family`.

### References
John Savard; quadibloc `pen03.htm`.

## "Pentagons and stars alone" — the open problem

**Summary.** The question of whether the plane can be tiled aperiodically by
a *finite* set of pieces that **all** have exact 5-fold symmetry. Unsolved.

| Property         | Value |
|------------------|-------|
| Symmetry order   | 5-fold |
| Symmetry type    | statistical (target) |
| Aperiodic        | target — no finite-tile solution known |
| Prototiles       | pentagon, star, decagon, … (all required to be 5-fold symmetric) |
| Construction     | substitution (target) |
| Inflation factor | n/a |
| Attribution      | Kepler (first attempt); Danzer, Grünbaum & Shephard (open status) |

### Prototiles
Pentagons, stars, decagons — every piece required to have exact 5-fold
rotational symmetry. The diamond and boat are *forbidden* (the diamond is not
5-fold symmetric; the boat is not either).

### Construction
Substitution would be the route. One can always tile with 5-fold-symmetric
pieces if allowed to add a new, larger piece *every* generation (the
"Dodecafoam" principle — an unbounded prototile set), producing patterns of
nested pentagons, stars, and decagons. The open question is whether a
**finite** prototile set suffices.

### Matching rules
None proposed — the obstruction is geometric, not a matching-rule gap.

### Symmetry & aperiodicity
Two adjacent decagons meeting a fat-star point still force a hexagon; five
decagons can surround a star, but over long range the tiling must behave like
a stars-and-pentagons-only tiling, which is impossible. So the all-5-fold
finite-tile tiling, if it exists, must be subtle.

### Variants & relations
Directly related to the boat-eliminated tiling (which removes the boat but
keeps the non-symmetric diamond) and to the HBS framework
(`pentagonal-binary.md`), where the hexagon plays the role the all-5-fold
program is trying to avoid.

### History & decoration
Kepler attempted this and "only got a little way before failing." The modern
open status is recorded by Danzer, Grünbaum & Shephard (1982, *American
Mathematical Monthly*), cited in Craig S. Kaplan's PhD thesis.

### Renderer mapping
Not a tiling — an open problem. No `Family`. Documented here so the renderer
roadmap does not chase an all-5-fold-symmetric `Family` that mathematics has
not delivered.

### References
Johannes Kepler; Danzer, Grünbaum & Shephard, *American Mathematical
Monthly*, 1982; Craig S. Kaplan, PhD thesis; quadibloc `pen04.htm`.
